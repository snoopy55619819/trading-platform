Trading Platform: ideal end state

Every service split along the lines the real industry draws, with no scope shortcuts. Compared with the current plan: SPLIT boxes were modules inside a merged process in the MVP, and dashed boxes and dotted lines were cut from the MVP entirely. A third plane, post-trade, appears here for the first time.

Key

  • Order plane
  • Market data plane
  • Post-trade plane
  • Connection cut from the MVP
  • Deployable process
  • Service cut from the MVP
  • Company boundary
  • SPLIT: a module in the MVP, its own service here

Legend

#Elementvs MVPWhat it does
1websameUnchanged: calls the broker for orders, market data for prices, joins them client-side.
2api-gatewaySPLITAuth, sessions, rate limits, request shaping. Scales horizontally with user traffic, which the OMS should not have to.
3omsSPLITOrder lifecycle and pre-trade risk. Splitting it from the ledger is the expensive one: the buying-power check becomes a reservation against the ledger rather than one shared transaction, so it needs idempotent fills and an outbox.
4sorPOST-MVPSmart order router. Splits and sweeps a parent order across venues by price, fee and latency. Cut from the MVP because with one venue it is a function that returns a constant.
5fix-engineSPLITOwns every FIX session, one per venue. Pulling it out is what lets the rest of the broker run as many replicas: the stateful singleton lives here and nowhere else.
6FIX to Alpha (edge)sameNewOrderSingle out, ExecutionReport back. The return path is private to the order's owner.
7exchange-betaPOST-MVPA second venue running the same exchange software with its own config and fee schedule. Its internals and return path mirror Alpha's, so it is drawn as one box.
8gatewaySPLITAlpha's order entry: sessions and validation. Separate so many gateways can front one engine, as real venues do.
9matching-engineSPLITAlpha's book. The one component that stays single-threaded and single-instance; everything around it is split so it does nothing but match.
10md-publisherSPLITAlpha's feed. Separate so a slow subscriber can never back-pressure the engine.
11sipPOST-MVPConsolidated tape. Merges every venue's feed into the national best bid and offer. Only meaningful once there are two venues.
12market-datasameNow consumes the consolidated NBBO instead of one venue's feed, and also serves it to the SOR.
13surveillancePOST-MVPRegulator. Receives a drop copy of every order and fill to look for wash trades, spoofing and layering.
14ccpPOST-MVPClearing house. Novation (becomes buyer to every seller), then end-of-day multilateral netting into a handful of obligations.
15csdPOST-MVPDepository and custodian: the actual record of who owns which shares. Moves them on settlement date.
16settlementPOST-MVPSettlement bank: the cash leg. Shares and cash move together or not at all (delivery versus payment).
17ledgerSPLITThe broker's back office. Books fills on trade date, moves them from unsettled to settled on the confirm from the depository.
18simulatorsameSynthetic trading firms. Now also arbitrages between Alpha and Beta, which is what keeps two independent books priced alike.
19clockPOST-MVPVirtual time. Fires end-of-day and settlement date so T+1 can be watched without waiting a real day.

Not shown on purpose: per-service databases (every service owns one; drawing ten more cylinders adds nothing), the fix-core library and the engine journal (unchanged from the current plan), Beta's internals and its trade reports to the CCP (identical to Alpha's), and the broker's event bus as a node (drawn as labelled edges instead).
